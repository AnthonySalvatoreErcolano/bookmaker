# Glossary additions for the Summa Theologica volumes.
# Use together with glossary.txt (the councils glossary); entries here replace any with the same name:
#   --glossary glossary.txt --glossary summa_glossary.txt
# Same format: term, optional "match:" line, then the definition; blank line between entries.
# A volume's glossary includes an entry only if the term appears in that volume.
# These definitions are editorial (not part of St Thomas's text); edit them freely.

Accident
match: accident | accidents | accidental*
That which exists in another as in a subject, not in itself: such as quantity, quality, relation, place or time. Contrasted with substance.

Act and potency
match: in act | in potentiality | potentiality | actuality | pure act
Potency (potentiality) is the capacity to be or to become something; act (actuality) is its realization. God, having no potentiality at all, is "pure act".

Analogy
match: analog* | univocal* | equivocal*
The way a word is said of different things in a sense partly the same and partly different. St Thomas holds that names are said of God and creatures neither univocally (in exactly the same sense) nor purely equivocally (in unrelated senses), but analogically.

Appetite
match: appetite* | appetitive
An inclination toward a good. The intellectual appetite is the will; the sensitive appetite is divided into the concupiscible and the irascible.

Article
match: =Article | =article
The basic unit of the Summa: a single question ("Whether…?") answered in a fixed order of objections, "On the contrary", "I answer that" and replies to the objections.

Beatitude
match: beatitude* | beatific
Perfect happiness, which St Thomas identifies with the vision of the divine essence.

Commentator, the
match: =Commentator | Averroes
Averroes (Ibn Rushd, 1126–1198), the great Arabic commentator on Aristotle.

Concupiscible and irascible
match: concupiscible | irascible
The two powers of the sensitive appetite: the concupiscible tends to sensible good and away from evil simply; the irascible meets good or evil that is difficult or arduous to obtain or avoid.

Contingent
match: contingent* | contingency
That which can be or not be. Contrasted with what is necessary.

Damascene
match: Damascene
St John of Damascus (c. 675–749), Greek theologian whose De Fide Orthodoxa St Thomas cites often.

Dionysius
match: Dionysius
The author of works written under the name of Dionysius the Areopagite (c. 500), On the Divine Names, The Celestial Hierarchy and others, much cited on God and the angels.

Efficient, final, formal and material causes
match: efficient cause* | final cause* | formal cause* | material cause* | exemplar cause*
Aristotle's four causes: the agent that produces something (efficient), the end for which it is done (final), what makes it the kind of thing it is (formal), and that out of which it is made (material). St Thomas adds the exemplar cause, the idea after which a thing is made.

Esse
match: esse | act of being | act of existence
The act of existing. In God essence and esse are identical; in every creature they are distinct.

Essence
match: essence* | quiddity | whatness
What a thing is; that by which it belongs to its species.

Form and matter
match: =form | substantial form* | prime matter | primary matter | hylomorph*
The two principles of a bodily substance: matter is the potential, determinable principle; form is the actual, determining principle that makes it what it is. The human soul is the substantial form of the body.

Habit
match: habit | habits | habitus
A stable quality disposing a power to act well or badly, such as a virtue or a vice.

I answer that
match: I answer that | respondeo
The body of an article (Latin respondeo dicendum), in which St Thomas gives his own solution.

Intelligible species
match: intelligible species | species
A likeness of a thing abstracted from sense images (phantasms) by the agent intellect, by which the intellect understands the thing.

Master, the
match: =Master | Master of the Sentences | Lombard
Peter Lombard (c. 1100–1160), author of the Sentences, the standard theological textbook of the Middle Ages.

Notional acts
match: notion* | notional
Acts or properties by which the divine Persons are distinguished: such as innascibility, paternity, filiation, spiration and procession.

Objection
match: Objection
An argument against the position St Thomas will defend, stated at the start of each article and answered at its end.

On the contrary
match: On the contrary | sed contra
A short argument from authority (Latin sed contra) indicating the position that St Thomas will defend.

Per se and per accidens
match: per se | per accidens | accidentally
Belonging to something of itself or by its nature (per se), as against belonging to it incidentally or through something else (per accidens).

Phantasm
match: phantasm*
A sense image produced by the imagination, from which the intellect abstracts intelligible species.

Philosopher, the
match: =Philosopher | Aristotle
Aristotle (384–322 BC), whom St Thomas cites simply as "the Philosopher".

Powers of the soul
match: powers of the soul | power of the soul | vegetative | sensitive | intellective
The faculties through which the soul acts: vegetative (nutrition, growth, reproduction), sensitive (the senses and sense appetite), and intellectual (intellect and will).

Precept
match: precept*
A command of law. St Thomas distinguishes the moral, ceremonial and judicial precepts of the Old Law.

Privation
match: privation*
The absence of something that ought to be present. Evil, for St Thomas, is a privation of good, not a positive thing.

Quodlibet
match: quodlibet*
A disputation in which a master answered questions on any subject put by those present; also the written record of such disputations.

Reply to Objection
match: Reply to Objection
The answers at the end of each article, in which St Thomas resolves each objection in the light of his solution.

Science (scientia)
match: =science | sciences | scientific knowledge
Certain knowledge through causes, reached by demonstration. Sacred doctrine is a science because it proceeds from principles known by revelation.

Spiration
match: spiration | spirated
The act by which the Father and the Son breathe forth the Holy Ghost.

Subsistence and suppositum
match: suppositum | supposit* | subsistence | subsist*
A suppositum is a complete individual substance existing on its own; a person is a suppositum of a rational nature. In Christ there is one suppositum, the divine Word, in two natures.

Substance
match: substance | substances | substantial
That which exists in itself and not in another. Contrasted with accident.

Synderesis
match: synderesis
The natural habit by which the first principles of moral action are known, such as "good is to be done and evil avoided".

Treatise
match: treatise
A group of questions on one subject, such as the Treatise on Law or the Treatise on the Incarnation.

Ubi
match: =ubi
"Where": the category of place.

Viator and comprehensor
match: wayfarer* | comprehensor* | viator*
A wayfarer (viator) is someone still on the way to God in this life; a comprehensor is one who has reached the end and sees God. St Thomas holds that Christ was both at once.
