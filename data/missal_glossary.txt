# Glossary for the Latin–English Missal volumes.
# Same format as glossary.txt: term, optional "match:" line, then the definition; blank line between entries.
# A volume's glossary includes an entry only if the term appears in that volume.
# These definitions are editorial; edit them freely.

Alleluia
match: Alleluia | Allelúja | Allelúia
A Hebrew acclamation, "Praise ye the Lord". In the Mass it follows the Gradual outside Septuagesima and Lent; in Eastertide it is added to many chants and replaces the Gradual with a double Alleluia.

Antiphon
match: antiphon* | =Ant.
A short text sung before and after a psalm or verse. The Introit, Offertory and Communion are antiphons.

Canon
match: =Canon | Canon Missæ | Canon of the Mass
The fixed central prayer of the Roman Mass, from the Te igitur to the doxology before the Pater noster, within which the Consecration takes place.

Classes (I–IV)
match: I. classis | II. classis | III. classis | IV. classis | first class | second class | third class | fourth class
The ranking of liturgical days introduced by the Code of Rubrics of 1960, replacing the older grades of double, semidouble and simple.

Collect
match: Collect | =Oratio
The principal prayer of the day, said by the priest after the Gloria (or the Kyrie), "collecting" the prayers of the people.

Commemoration
match: commemorat* | =Commemoratio
A second or further Collect, Secret and Postcommunion added on a day when another feast or season also falls, so that it is remembered though its own Mass is not said.

Common
match: =Commune | =Common | Commune Sanctorum
A Mass written for a class of saints (Apostles, Martyrs, Confessors, Virgins and so on), used for saints who have no complete Mass of their own.

Communion
match: =Communio | Communion
The antiphon sung during or after the distribution of Holy Communion.

Confiteor
match: Confiteor | Confíteor
The general confession of sins made at the foot of the altar and, in the older rubrics, before Holy Communion.

Credo
match: Credo | Nicene Creed
The Nicene Creed, said after the Gospel on Sundays, on feasts of the first class and on certain other days.

Divino Afflatu
match: Divino Afflatu
The apostolic constitution of St Pius X (1911) that reformed the Breviary and with it the rubrics of the Missal.

Double, semidouble, simple
match: =Duplex | Semiduplex | =Simplex | double of the first class | double of the second class | greater double | semidouble
The older grades of liturgical days before 1960, deciding which day's Mass is said when two coincide: duplex I. or II. classis, duplex majus, duplex, semiduplex and simplex.

Ember days
match: Ember* | Quattuor Temporum | Quatuor Temporum
The Wednesday, Friday and Saturday of four weeks of the year (in Advent, in Lent, after Pentecost and in September), kept with fasting and prayer and, on the Saturday, with several lessons.

Epistle
match: Epistle | Epístola | Lectio
The first reading of the Mass, usually from the letters of the Apostles, read from the south side of the altar.

Feria
match: =Feria | ferial | ferias
A weekday on which no feast is kept. In the older rubrics the Mass of a feria with no proper Mass is that of the preceding Sunday.

Gloria
match: =Gloria | Glória in excélsis | Gloria in excelsis
The hymn "Glory to God in the highest", said after the Kyrie on Sundays outside Advent and Septuagesima to Lent, on feasts and in their octaves.

Gospel
match: =Evangelium | =Gospel
The reading from the Gospels, the climax of the Mass of the Catechumens, read from the north side of the altar.

Gradual
match: =Graduale | Gradual
The chant between the Epistle and the Gospel, originally sung from the steps (gradus) of the ambo.

Holy Week (restored)
match: Ordo Hebdomadæ Sanctæ | restored Holy Week | Instaurata
The reformed rites of Holy Week issued in 1955 (Maxima Redemptionis), with the Easter Vigil restored to the night; earlier versions keep the older rites.

Introit
match: =Introitus | Introit
The entrance chant of the Mass: an antiphon, a psalm verse and the Gloria Patri, with the antiphon repeated.

Kyrie
match: Kyrie | Kýrie
The ninefold "Lord, have mercy; Christ, have mercy; Lord, have mercy".

Last Gospel
match: Last Gospel | Initium sancti Evangelii secundum Joannem | In princípio erat Verbum
The beginning of St John's Gospel, read at the end of Mass; on certain days another Gospel was read instead before 1960.

Octave
match: octav* | =Octava
The eight-day prolongation of a great feast, ending with the octave day. Most octaves were suppressed in 1955.

Offertory
match: =Offertorium | Offertory
The antiphon said as the bread and wine are offered, and by extension the offering prayers themselves.

Order of Mass
match: Ordo Missæ | Order of Mass | Ordinary of the Mass
The unchanging parts of the Mass, into which the proper texts of the day are inserted.

Postcommunion
match: Postcommunio | Postcommunion
The prayer said after Holy Communion, answering the Collect and the Secret.

Preface
match: =Præfatio | =Prefatio | Preface | Præfátio
The solemn thanksgiving that opens the Canon, ending with the Sanctus. Proper prefaces are said on certain feasts and seasons.

Proper
match: =Proprium | Proper of the Season | Proper of the Saints | Proprium de Tempore | Proprium Sanctorum
The texts that change from day to day (Introit, Collect, Epistle, Gradual, Gospel, Offertory, Secret, Communion, Postcommunion). The Proper of the Season follows the liturgical year; the Proper of the Saints follows the calendar.

Rogation days
match: Rogation* | Litaniæ majores | Litaniæ minores
Days of supplication, with a procession and the Litany of the Saints: St Mark's day (the Greater Litanies) and the three days before the Ascension (the Lesser Litanies).

Rubric
match: rubric*
A direction for the conduct of the rite, printed in red (ruber) to distinguish it from the words to be said.

Secret
match: =Secreta | =Secret
The prayer over the offerings, said silently by the priest before the Preface.

Septuagesima
match: Septuagesim* | Sexagesim* | Quinquagesim*
The three Sundays before Lent and the weeks they begin, a time of preparation in which the Gloria and the Alleluia are no longer said.

Sequence
match: =Sequentia | Sequence
A rhymed hymn sung after the Alleluia on certain days: Victimae paschali (Easter), Veni Sancte Spiritus (Pentecost), Lauda Sion (Corpus Christi), Stabat Mater (the Seven Sorrows) and Dies irae (Masses for the dead).

Station
match: Statio | =Station
The Roman church where the Pope and people once gathered for the Mass of the day, noted at the head of many Masses of Lent and other seasons.

Tract
match: =Tractus | =Tract
Verses of a psalm sung without refrain after the Gradual, in place of the Alleluia, from Septuagesima to Easter and in Masses for the dead.

Vigil
match: Vigil* | =Vigilia
The day before a great feast, kept as a day of preparation, often with its own Mass.

Votive Mass
match: votiv* | =Votiva
A Mass said by choice, out of devotion or for a particular need, rather than the Mass of the day.
