# Glossary for the Councils volumes
#
# Each entry: the term on its own line, an optional "match:" line, then the definition.
# Leave a blank line between entries.
#   match:  words to look for in the text, separated by | (same rules as index_terms.txt:
#           "*" = any ending, "=" = exact capitals). Without a match: line the term itself is used.
# A volume's glossary includes an entry only if the term appears in that volume.
# These definitions are editorial (not part of the council texts); edit them freely.

Absolution
match: absolution | absolve*
In the sacrament of penance, the words by which a priest declares the penitent's sins forgiven.

Anathema
match: anathem* | anathematis* | anathematiz*
A solemn condemnation that cuts a person off from the communion of the Church. In conciliar canons the formula "let him be anathema" attaches this sentence to anyone who holds the doctrine being condemned.

Annates
The first year's revenues of a benefice, paid to the papal treasury by the person appointed to it.

Antipope
match: antipope*
A person who claimed or exercised the office of pope in opposition to the one regarded by the Church as the lawful pope.

Apocrisiarius
match: apocrisiari*
An envoy representing the pope or a patriarch at the imperial court in Constantinople; a forerunner of the later papal nuncio.

Apollinarianism
match: Apollinar*
The teaching of Apollinarius of Laodicea (4th century) that in Christ the divine Word took the place of a human mind, so that Christ was not fully human. Condemned at Constantinople I (381).

Apostolate
match: apostolate*
The mission of spreading the Gospel and sanctifying the world. Vatican II taught that all the faithful, lay people as well as clergy, share in it.

Apostolic see
match: apostolic see
The see of Rome, as founded on the apostles Peter and Paul; by extension, the pope and the officials acting in his name.

Archdeacon
match: archdeacon*
A senior cleric who assisted the bishop in governing a diocese, especially in matters of discipline and church property.

Archimandrite
match: archimandrite*
In the Eastern churches, the head of a large monastery or of a group of monasteries.

Arianism
match: Arius | Arian*
The teaching of the Alexandrian priest Arius (d. 336) that the Son of God was created by the Father and was therefore neither eternal nor fully divine. Condemned at Nicaea I (325).

Attrition
match: attrition
Sorrow for sin arising from motives such as fear of punishment, as distinguished from contrition. Trent taught that it disposes the sinner to receive grace in the sacrament of penance.

Beguines and Beghards
match: Beguin* | Beghard*
Communities of laywomen (Beguines) and laymen (Beghards) who lived a religious life without taking permanent vows. Their way of life was restricted at the Council of Vienne (1311–1312).

Benefice
match: benefice*
A church office that carries a permanent right to an income, usually from endowed property, such as a parish or a canonry.

Breviary
match: breviar*
The book containing the Divine Office, the daily cycle of prayer said by clergy and religious. Trent entrusted its revision to the pope.

Bull
match: bull | bulls
A solemn papal document, named after the lead seal (Latin bulla) attached to it.

Canon
match: canon | canons
(1) A rule or decree of church law, especially one issued by a council. (2) A member of the chapter of a cathedral or collegiate church. (3) The canon of Scripture: the list of books recognised as inspired.

Cardinal
match: cardinal*
A senior cleric of the Roman Church who advises the pope and, since 1059, takes part in electing him.

Catechumen
match: catechumen*
A person preparing for baptism through instruction and a period of probation.

Cathars
match: =Cathar* | Albigensian*
(1) The name ("the pure") used by the Novatianists, a rigorist schism of the early Church, as in canon 8 of Nicaea I. (2) The dualist heretics of 12th- and 13th-century southern France and Italy, also called Albigensians, condemned at Lateran III and Lateran IV.

Celibacy
match: celibacy | celibate
The obligation of clergy in major orders in the Latin Church to remain unmarried.

Chapter
match: chapter of the cathedral | cathedral chapter* | chapters of cathedral* | cathedral and collegiate
The body of canons attached to a cathedral or collegiate church, responsible for its worship and at times for electing the bishop.

Chorepiscopus
match: chorepiscop*
In the early Church, a "country bishop" who served rural districts under the authority of the bishop of the city.

Chrism
match: chrism
Consecrated olive oil mixed with balsam, used in baptism, confirmation and ordination.

Clandestine marriage
match: clandestine
A marriage contracted secretly, without the public form required by the Church. Trent's decree Tametsi required marriage to be celebrated before the parish priest and witnesses.

Collegiality
match: college of bishops | episcopal college | collegial*
The teaching of Vatican II that the bishops, together with the pope as their head and never without him, form a college that shares responsibility for the whole Church.

Commendam
match: commend*
The holding of a benefice "in trust" by someone who drew its revenues without residing in it or carrying out its duties; frequently criticised as an abuse.

Communion under both kinds
match: both species | both kinds | under one species | under the species of bread
Receiving the Eucharist as both bread and wine. Constance and Trent upheld the practice of giving lay people communion under the form of bread alone.

Conciliarism
match: superior to the pope | authority immediately from Christ | conciliar*
The theory that a general council holds authority superior to that of the pope; associated with the councils of Constance and Basel.

Concubinage
match: concubin*
A lasting sexual relationship outside marriage. Councils legislated repeatedly against concubinage among the clergy.

Concupiscence
match: concupiscence
The inclination to sin that remains after baptism. Trent taught that it comes from sin and inclines to sin, but is not itself sin in the baptised.

Confirmation
match: sacrament of confirmation | confirmation | #19-21
The sacrament in which the baptised are anointed with chrism and sealed with the Holy Spirit.

Consanguinity
match: consanguinity | affinity
Blood relationship, which in certain degrees is an impediment to marriage. Affinity is the corresponding relationship created by marriage.

Consubstantial
match: consubstantial | homoousios | homoousion | one substance
"Of one substance" (Greek homoousios): the term used at Nicaea I to affirm that the Son shares the same divine being as the Father.

Contrition
match: contrition | contrite
Sorrow for sin, together with the resolve not to sin again, arising from love of God.

Curia
match: curia*
The offices and officials who assist the pope (the Roman curia) or a bishop in governing the Church.

Deacon
match: deacon | deacons | diaconate
An ordained minister of the order below bishop and presbyter, traditionally assisting in the liturgy and in the care of the poor.

Deaconess
match: deaconess*
In the early Church, a woman appointed to ministries such as assisting at the baptism of women. Nicaea I classed the deaconesses of the Paulianists among the laity.

Diocese
match: diocese*
The territory and the faithful entrusted to a bishop.

Dispensation
match: dispensat*
The relaxation of a church law in a particular case by a competent authority.

Divine Office
match: divine office | canonical hours | the hours
The Church's daily public prayer at fixed hours (the canonical hours), recited by clergy and religious; also called the Liturgy of the Hours.

Dogmatic constitution
match: dogmatic constitution*
The title given to the most solemn doctrinal documents of Vatican I and Vatican II, such as Dei Filius, Lumen Gentium and Dei Verbum.

Ecumenical council
match: ecumenical council* | general council*
A gathering of the bishops of the whole Church whose decrees, approved by the pope, bind the whole Church. The twenty-one councils in these volumes are those counted as ecumenical by the Catholic Church.

Ecumenism
match: ecumenism | ecumenical movement
The movement for restoring unity among divided Christians, the subject of Vatican II's decree Unitatis Redintegratio.

Episcopal conference
match: episcopal conference*
The assembly of the bishops of a country or region acting together, given a formal role by Vatican II.

Eucharist
match: eucharist*
The sacrament of the body and blood of Christ, offered in the Mass and received in Holy Communion.

Eutychianism
match: Eutyches | Eutychian*
The teaching of the monk Eutyches that after the incarnation Christ had only one nature, the human being absorbed into the divine. Condemned at Chalcedon (451). See also Monophysitism.

Ex cathedra
match: ex cathedra
"From the chair": describes a definition by the pope, acting as supreme teacher of the Church, of a doctrine of faith or morals to be held by the whole Church. Vatican I declared such definitions infallible.

Exarch
match: exarch*
In the early Eastern Church, a senior bishop with authority over several provinces; later also a deputy of a patriarch.

Excommunication
match: excommunicat*
Exclusion from the communion of the Church, especially from the sacraments, imposed as a penalty for grave offences.

Exemption
match: exemption* | exempt
The release of a person or institution, usually a religious order or monastery, from the authority of the local bishop, placing it directly under the pope.

Extreme unction
match: extreme unction | unction of the sick
The sacrament of anointing the seriously ill with oil, now usually called the anointing of the sick.

Filioque
match: Filioque | from the Father and the Son | from the Father and from the Son
"And from the Son": the Latin addition to the creed stating that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father and the Son. A long-standing point of dispute with the Eastern churches, addressed at Lyons II and Florence.

Free will
match: free will | free-will | freedom of the will
The human capacity to choose. Against the Reformers, Trent affirmed that it was weakened, but not destroyed, by the fall.

Grace
match: grace
God's free gift; in Catholic teaching especially the gift by which God makes a person just and able to live a holy life (sanctifying or justifying grace).

Holy orders
match: holy orders | sacrament of order | ordination
The sacrament by which bishops, priests and deacons are ordained.

Hypostasis
match: hypostas* | hypostatic*
Greek for a concrete individual reality; in the doctrine of the Trinity, a "person" (three hypostases in one substance). The hypostatic union is the union of the divine and human natures in the one person of Christ.

Iconoclasm
match: iconoclas*
The 8th- and 9th-century movement that rejected and destroyed religious images. Condemned at Nicaea II (787).

Indiction
match: indiction
In council documents, the formal announcement of the date of a council or of its next session. (Also the name of a fifteen-year cycle used in dating documents.)

Index of Forbidden Books
match: index of books | index of prohibited books | forbidden books | prohibited books
The list of books that Catholics were forbidden to read or keep. Trent entrusted its preparation to the pope.

Indulgence
match: indulgence*
A remission, granted by the Church, of the temporal punishment still due for sins whose guilt has already been forgiven.

Infallibility
match: infallib*
The gift by which the Church, and the pope when defining doctrine ex cathedra, is preserved from error in defining doctrine of faith or morals (Vatican I, Pastor Aeternus).

Interdict
match: interdict*
A church penalty that forbids most sacraments and public worship in a place, or bars certain persons from them.

Investiture
match: investiture | lay hands
The conferring of a church office together with its symbols. The investiture of bishops by lay rulers was contested in the 11th and 12th centuries and settled by the Concordat of Worms (1122), confirmed at Lateran I.

Justification
match: justif*
The passage of a sinner from a state of sin to a state of grace and righteousness before God; the subject of Trent's sixth session.

Laity
match: laity | lay people | laymen | lay persons
All the faithful who are not ordained; in Vatican II's usage, also distinct from members of religious orders.

Lapsed
match: lapsed
In the early Church, Christians who had denied the faith under persecution.

Legate
match: legate*
A representative of the pope, often presiding at councils in his name.

Liturgy
match: liturg*
The public worship of the Church, above all the Mass and the sacraments.

Magisterium
match: magisterium | teaching authority | teaching office
The teaching authority of the Church, exercised by the pope and by the bishops in communion with him.

Manichees
match: Manichae* | Manichee*
Followers of the religion founded by Mani (3rd century), which taught two opposing first principles of good and evil. The name was later applied to dualist heresies generally.

Matrimony
match: matrimon*
The sacrament of marriage, defined by Trent as one of the seven sacraments.

Mendicant orders
match: mendicant* | friars
Religious orders that lived by begging rather than from endowments, notably the Franciscans and Dominicans.

Merit
match: merit | merits | meritorious
The reward due to good works. Trent taught that the good works of the justified, done in grace, truly merit eternal life.

Messalians
match: Messalian* | Euchites
An ascetic movement (also called Euchites, "those who pray") which held that prayer alone, not the sacraments, could free the soul from evil. Condemned at Ephesus (431).

Metropolitan
match: metropolitan*
The bishop of the chief city (metropolis) of an ecclesiastical province, with oversight of its other bishops; later usually called an archbishop.

Missal
match: missal
The book containing the prayers and directions for celebrating Mass. Trent entrusted its revision to the pope.

Monophysitism
match: monophysit* | one nature | #3-8
The doctrine that after the incarnation Christ has only one nature, rejected by Chalcedon's definition of two natures in one person.

Monothelitism
match: monothelet* | monothelit* | one will | #6-8
The 7th-century teaching that Christ had only one will. Constantinople III (680–681) rejected it and defined two natural wills in Christ.

Monte di pietà
match: monte* di pietà | mons pietatis | montes pietatis | monti di pietà
A charitable pawnshop lending to the poor at small charges. Lateran V declared such institutions lawful.

Nestorianism
match: Nestorius | Nestorian*
The teaching attributed to Nestorius, bishop of Constantinople, that the divine and the human in Christ were two distinct subjects, expressed in his rejection of the title Theotokos. Condemned at Ephesus (431).

Ordinary
match: local ordinar* | the ordinary | ordinaries
A cleric with jurisdiction by virtue of his office rather than by delegation, above all the bishop of a diocese ("local ordinary").

Origenism
match: Origen | Origenist*
Ideas attributed to Origen (3rd century) and his later followers, such as the pre-existence of souls and a final restoration of all things, condemned in the 6th century.

Original sin
match: original sin
The state of sin into which all human beings are born as a consequence of Adam's sin, removed by baptism. Defined at Trent's fifth session.

Ousia
match: ousia*
Greek for "substance" or "essence"; in the doctrine of the Trinity, the one divine being shared by Father, Son and Holy Spirit.

Pallium
match: pallium
A band of white wool worn by the pope and conferred by him on metropolitan archbishops as a sign of their authority and of communion with Rome.

Patriarch
match: patriarch*
The bishop of one of the principal sees of Christendom (Rome, Constantinople, Alexandria, Antioch and Jerusalem), with authority over a wide region; also the head of certain Eastern churches.

Paulianists
match: Paulianist* | Paulinist* | Paul of Samosata
Followers of Paul of Samosata (3rd century), who denied the distinct divinity of the Son. Nicaea I required them to be rebaptised.

Penance
match: penance
The sacrament by which sins committed after baptism are forgiven, through contrition, confession, satisfaction and absolution; also the works of satisfaction themselves.

Penitential grades
match: hearers | prostrators | kneelers | weepers
The stages through which public penitents passed in the early Church before full readmission: weepers, hearers (who left after the readings), prostrators or kneelers, and those who stood with the faithful but did not receive communion.

People of God
match: People of God
Vatican II's image of the Church as the people whom God has gathered in Christ, made up of all the faithful.

Pluralism
match: plurality of benefices | several benefices | pluralit*
The holding of several benefices at once by one person, repeatedly restricted by councils.

Pneumatomachians
match: Pneumatomachi* | Macedonian*
"Fighters against the Spirit", also called Macedonians: those who denied the divinity of the Holy Spirit. Condemned at Constantinople I (381).

Pragmatic Sanction
match: pragmatic sanction
The Pragmatic Sanction of Bourges (1438), by which the French crown limited papal rights in France. Condemned and annulled at Lateran V.

Prebend
match: prebend*
The share of a cathedral's revenues assigned to a canon; by extension, the canonry itself.

Predestination
match: predestinat*
God's eternal decree ordering the elect to salvation. Trent warned against presuming that one is among the predestined.

Presbyter
match: presbyter*
An "elder": a priest, of the order between bishop and deacon.

Primacy
match: primacy
The authority of the pope, as successor of Peter, over the whole Church, defined at Vatican I.

Primate
match: primate*
A bishop with precedence over the other bishops of a country or region.

Provincial synod
match: provincial synod* | provincial council*
A meeting of the bishops of an ecclesiastical province under their metropolitan, which councils required to be held at regular intervals.

Purgatory
match: purgator*
The state of purification after death of those who die in God's grace but still need cleansing before entering heaven.

Quartodecimans
match: quartodeciman* | fourteenth day
Christians who kept Easter on the 14th day of the Jewish month of Nisan, whatever the day of the week, rather than on the following Sunday.

Regular and secular clergy
match: regulars | secular clergy | secular clerics | regular clergy | seculars
Regular clergy live under a religious rule (Latin regula), such as monks, friars and canons regular. Secular clergy serve "in the world" under a bishop, such as parish priests.

Relics
match: relics
The bodily remains of saints, or objects associated with them, venerated by the faithful.

Religious
match: religious orders | religious life | the religious
As a noun, a member of a religious order or congregation who has taken vows.

Reservation
match: reserved cases | reservation* | reserv* of cases
The keeping of certain matters to a higher authority: for example the absolution of certain grave sins (reserved cases), or the appointment to certain benefices.

Residence
match: residence | reside
The obligation of bishops and other pastors to live in the place of their office, strongly reinforced at Trent.

Rite
match: rite | rites
A liturgical and disciplinary tradition within the Church, such as the Latin (Roman) rite or the various Eastern rites.

Sabellianism
match: Sabellius | Sabellian*
The teaching of Sabellius (3rd century) that Father, Son and Holy Spirit are only successive modes or names of one divine person.

Sacrament
match: sacrament | sacraments
An outward sign instituted by Christ that confers grace. Florence and Trent taught that there are seven: baptism, confirmation, Eucharist, penance, extreme unction, holy orders and matrimony.

Satisfaction
match: satisfaction
The works (such as prayer, fasting and almsgiving) by which a penitent makes amends for sin; a part of the sacrament of penance.

Schism
match: schism*
A break in the communion of the Church, which need not involve a difference of doctrine.

See
match: =see | =See of | episcopal see* | the see
The seat of a bishop's authority; by extension, the diocese itself.

Seminary
match: seminar*
A college for the training of priests. Trent ordered every diocese to establish one.

Session
match: session*
A formal meeting of a council, at which its decrees were read out and approved.

Simony
match: simon* | simoniac*
The buying or selling of spiritual things, especially church offices and sacraments; named after Simon Magus (Acts 8:18–24).

Suffragan
match: suffragan*
A bishop of a diocese within a province, subject to its metropolitan.

Synod
match: synod*
A council or assembly of bishops. In these texts the words "synod" and "council" are used interchangeably.

Templars
match: Templar* | Order of the Temple | #15
The Knights Templar, a military religious order founded in the 12th century to protect pilgrims to the Holy Land. Suppressed by Pope Clement V at the Council of Vienne (1312).

Theotokos
match: Theotokos | mother of God | God-bearer
"God-bearer" or Mother of God: the title of Mary affirmed at Ephesus (431).

Three Chapters
match: Three Chapters
Writings of Theodore of Mopsuestia, Theodoret of Cyrrhus and Ibas of Edessa, condemned at Constantinople II (553).

Tithe
match: tithe*
The tenth part of produce or income owed to the Church for the support of the clergy and the poor.

Tradition
match: tradition*
The teaching handed down in the Church from the apostles, alongside Scripture. Trent spoke of "unwritten traditions", Vatican II of Sacred Tradition.

Transubstantiation
match: transubstantiat*
The change of the whole substance of the bread and wine into the body and blood of Christ in the Eucharist, while the appearances of bread and wine remain. The term appears at Lateran IV and was defined at Trent.

Usury
match: usur*
In medieval canon law, the taking of any profit on a loan; forbidden to clergy at Nicaea I and later to all Christians.

Vernacular
match: vernacular | mother tongue
The everyday language of a people, as distinct from Latin. Vatican II allowed its wider use in the liturgy.

Viaticum
match: viaticum
Holy Communion given to the dying, as "provision for the journey".

Vulgate
match: Vulgate
The Latin translation of the Bible made largely by St Jerome, declared authentic for public use by Trent.

Waldensians
match: Waldens* | Poor of Lyons
Followers of Valdes of Lyons (12th century), a lay movement of voluntary poverty and preaching, later condemned as heretical.
